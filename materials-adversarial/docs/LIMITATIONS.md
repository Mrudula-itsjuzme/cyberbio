# Critical Scientific and Technical Limitations

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Explicit Thesis Positioning Statement**:
> *"This work is a proof-of-concept for constrained adversarial robustness in materials sequence modelling, not a claim of complete chemical realism or universally improved predictive performance."*

Scientific integrity requires transparently identifying framework limitations. The following 15 points outline the boundary conditions, assumptions, and constraints of this research:

1. **Lack of Independent Physical Oracle for Chemistry-Changing Edits**: While the Rand-SMILES augmentation inherits physical labels securely, the MCMC defense generates chemistry-changing perturbations. Because we do not recalculate the ground-truth band gap of these new molecules using Density Functional Theory (DFT) or an independent property oracle, the MCMC defense provides *label-free robustness regularization* (reducing model sensitivity) rather than establishing *improved physical accuracy* on the novel candidates.
1. **Sequence Length Shortcut Exploitation**: PSMILES sequence length has a strong negative correlation ($r = -0.4310$) with polymer bandgap ($E_g$). A simple 1D linear model on sequence length alone achieves $R^2 = 0.2294$ ($\text{RMSE} = 1.2325\text{ eV}$). The Transformer model partially exploits string length as a shortcut feature.
2. **Scaffold Leakage under Random Splitting**: Under default random splitting (`strategy: random` in `configs/dataset.yaml`), **110 out of 238 test scaffolds (46.2%)** overlap with the training set. True out-of-scaffold generalization requires scaffold-grouped splitting (`strategy: scaffold`).
3. **Representation Sensitivity Vulnerability**: Querying non-canonical, chemically identical SMILES representations for the exact same molecule causes a **Mean Absolute Drift of 0.6147 eV** (Max Drift = $1.3622\text{ eV}$). The model is sensitive to string syntax rather than pure 2D graph topology.
4. **Computational Plausibility vs. Wet-Lab Synthesizability**: RDKit validity, valence correctness, attachment point star `*` balance, molecular weight bounds ($\pm 50\%$), and Tanimoto similarity ($S_{\text{Tanimoto}} \ge 0.5$) prove **computational plausibility** in SMILES space. They do **not** guarantee physical lab synthesizability (Synthetic Accessibility / SA score), reaction pathway feasibility, or thermodynamic/kinetic stability.
5. **Clean Accuracy vs. Adversarial Robustness Tradeoff**: Adversarial training increases Clean RMSE from $1.1439 \pm 0.1128\text{ eV}$ to $1.3672 \pm 0.1631\text{ eV}$ on the 20-sample benchmark slice ($+0.2233\text{ eV}$ error increase). Smoothing local loss landscapes for robust predictions incurs a Pareto tradeoff against peak clean distribution fit.
6. **Absolute Magnitude of Epistemic Uncertainty Shift**: While epistemic uncertainty drift drops by **83.33%** after defender training, the absolute uncertainty magnitude drops from $0.0006 \pm 0.0008\text{ eV}$ to $0.0001 \pm 0.0002\text{ eV}$ ($0.0005\text{ eV}$ absolute shift). Epistemic variance values are small ($\sim 10^{-4}\text{ eV}$), reflecting general Transformer overconfidence across sequence perturbations.
7. **Threat Surface Scope**: The threat model targets digital screening pipelines where automated AI models process polymer sequence strings. An adversarial prediction drift measures model prediction fragility under digital sequence manipulation, not a physical biothreat or physical material destruction.
8. **Query Budget Constraint ($Q=20$)**: All attacks are evaluated under a strict query budget of $Q \le 20$ queries per sample. Highly unconstrained attackers ($Q \ge 1000$) with unlimited model access might discover larger prediction drift.
9. **5-Seed Statistical Sample**: Evaluated across 5 random seeds ($42, 123, 2026, 777, 999$). While 5 seeds demonstrate significant variance reduction (78.5% drop in cross-seed drift variance), broader sweeps (10-20 seeds) would provide even tighter confidence intervals.
10. **Simplified Modification Operators**: Discrete attack proposals utilize basic atom substitutions, insertions, deletions, and functional group isosteres. Complex multi-ring scission or macrocyclic rearrangements are excluded.
11. **Single-Property Primary Evaluation ($E_g$)**: Primary evaluations focus on solid-state polymer bandgap prediction ($E_g$). Other properties (e.g. glass transition temperature $T_g$, dielectric constant) require property-specific validation.
12. **MC-Dropout Epistemic Approximation**: MC-Dropout provides a scalable approximation of epistemic variance, but is less exact than full Bayesian Neural Network (BNN) Markov chain sampling.
13. **Single-Architecture Primary Defense**: Primary adversarial training is performed on the sequence Transformer regressor; cross-model transferability to GNNs (GraphMPNN) represents an independent evaluation path.
14. **Tanimoto Similarity Threshold Sensitivity**: Results depend on $S_{\text{Tanimoto}} \ge 0.5$. Lower cutoffs allow off-target chemical drift, while higher cutoffs restrict search freedom.
15. **Solid-State vs. Gas-Phase Gap Confounding**: While solid-state bandgap $E_g$ is the target, proxy datasets derived from gas-phase HOMO-LUMO calculations introduce systematic dielectric screening offsets ($1.0 - 2.0\text{ eV}$).
